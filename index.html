      How it works, and what it cannot do

      The steps

      • Detections from MODIS and VIIRS are counted per day for your area. Low-confidence detections are dropped by default.
      • On the days both sensors were flying, we compare total counts. The ratio is the scale factor that turns VIIRS counts into MODIS-equivalent counts. Where both sensors cover a day we average MODIS and scaled VIIRS.
      • For every week we measure sensor agreement from two things: how close the two counts are after scaling, and how many 0.25 degree map cells where MODIS saw fire VIIRS also saw. That becomes the trust level.
      • Each week is compared with the same week in other years. A week is flagged when it is well above the usual spread.
      • The season shift is the date when half of a season's detections have happened. We fit a straight line through the years.

      Limits to keep in mind

      • This is a prototype, not a validated scientific product. The scaling is one simple ratio for the whole area, not a physical model.
      • The trust thresholds (high from 0.70, medium from 0.45) are provisional.
      • Satellites only see fires when they pass over. Fires lit in the evening can be missed, so a drop in counts does not prove a drop in burning.
      • A trend line through a few years can be chance. The app tells you when it cannot rule that out.
      • The sample data on the first screen is synthetic. It only shows how the tool behaves.
